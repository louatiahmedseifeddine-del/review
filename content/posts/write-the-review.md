The useful review is not a verdict. It is a record of what the thing actually did to your attention while you were still inside it.

A week later, memory tidies the experience. The slow parts shrink. The one sharp sentence gets promoted to the whole point. If you wait for a finished opinion, you end up reviewing the story you told yourself, not the book, the interface, or the week of work.

I keep the notes small on purpose:

- what I expected
- what surprised me
- what I would change
- one line I do not want to lose

That is enough to write from. The essay can come later. The review has to happen while the details are still slightly inconvenient.

> A review written from memory is a review of your memory.

This journal is that habit, made public. Short pieces. Dated. Willing to be incomplete, as long as they are specific.
